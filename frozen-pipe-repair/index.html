Frozen Pipe Repair in Union, NJ

Union's humid continental climate brings real winter freeze risk between December and March. We handle both emergency thawing and the repair after a freeze break.

Union Township sees genuine winter freeze conditions each year, and pipes in unheated crawlspaces, garages, and along exterior walls in older homes are the most exposed. A frozen pipe that hasn't burst yet is an emergency in its own right, since a full freeze can crack the pipe from internal ice pressure even before any water is visible.

If you've lost water flow to a fixture during a cold snap and suspect a freeze, shutting off the main water valve and calling before attempting to thaw the pipe yourself is the safer move, since an already-cracked pipe will start leaking heavily the moment it thaws.

Where Pipes Freeze

Highest-Risk Locations In Union Homes

Unheated Crawlspaces

Common in older Union Township homes, crawlspace pipe runs are directly exposed to outdoor temperature swings.

Exterior Wall Runs

Pipes routed through poorly insulated exterior walls, especially in additions or older construction.

Garages

Unheated attached garages with a supply line running through them see repeated freeze risk each winter.

Attic Runs

Less common but present in some homes, attic pipe runs face similar exposure to crawlspaces.

Safe Thawing

Why We Don't Recommend DIY Thawing With Open Flame

A propane torch or other open flame near a frozen pipe is a real fire risk, especially near wood framing in a crawlspace or wall cavity. We use controlled heat sources, heat tape, or hot water application to thaw a pipe safely, checking for existing cracks before full water pressure returns. A pipe that's already cracked from the freeze is a job for burst pipe repair once it's safely thawed.

Prevention

Reducing Next Winter's Risk

Pipe insulation sleeves on exposed runs, sealing crawlspace vents during the coldest months, and keeping a trickle of water running through vulnerable fixtures during a hard freeze all reduce risk. We can identify a home's specific freeze-prone points during a service visit and recommend targeted insulation rather than guessing. A basement with a sump pump is worth checking at the same time, since winter freeze-ups and spring flooding are two ends of the same seasonal risk.

The Real Cost Of Waiting

Why A Small Fix Now Beats A Big One Later

Insulating a handful of exposed pipe runs ahead of winter typically costs a fraction of what a single freeze-break emergency call runs, before even factoring in any water damage from the burst itself. Homes that have had one freeze break are statistically more likely to have another at a different point along the same exposed run, since the underlying exposure that caused the first break, whether that's a gap in crawlspace insulation or an underinsulated exterior wall, hasn't gone away just because one section got fixed.

For homes in Union Township with a documented history of winter freeze issues, we can walk the full exposed pipe path during a single visit and flag every vulnerable point at once, rather than addressing them one emergency call at a time across multiple winters.
