A dead water heater in Union rarely waits for a convenient time. Many homes in this part of Union County are pre-war and mid-century builds, and the water heaters inside them are often older units nearing the end of a typical 8 to 12 year service life. When one fails, the first question is usually whether it can be repaired that day or needs to be replaced outright, and getting a straight answer on the phone matters more than a sales pitch.

We repair both tank and tankless water heaters, gas and electric, and we start with a real diagnosis rather than a default part swap. Most no-heat and leaking-tank calls in Union Township come down to a short list of causes, and naming them upfront on the visit saves a second trip.

Common Causes

Why Union Homes Lose Hot Water

Failed Heating Element or Thermocouple

Electric units lose one of two elements; gas units often fail at the thermocouple or igniter, not the whole tank.

Sediment Buildup

Older Union homes on municipal water see mineral buildup at the tank bottom, which insulates the burner and causes rumbling and slow recovery.

Leaking Tank Seams or Fittings

A puddle at the base of the tank, especially in an older basement unit, usually means a compromised tank or a loose fitting worth checking before assuming full replacement.

Tripped High-Limit Switch

Electric water heaters with a tripped high-limit switch cut power as a safety measure and need diagnosis before a simple reset is safe.

How We Diagnose It

Finding The Real Problem First

A technician checks gas supply and igniter function on gas units, or tests both heating elements and the thermostat on electric units, before recommending a fix. For tankless systems we check the flow sensor, heat exchanger, and any error code on the unit's display, since tankless faults are almost always electronic rather than mechanical.

If the tank itself has failed, we say so plainly and quote a same-day replacement rather than patching a unit that will fail again within weeks. If it is a repairable part, we carry common thermocouples, elements, and igniters on the truck so most repairs finish in one visit.

Repair vs Replace

Making The Right Call

Repair Makes Sense When

  • Unit is under 8 years old
  • Failure is a single part, not the tank
  • No active leak from the tank body
  • Efficiency and recovery time are still normal

Replacement Makes Sense When

  • Tank is leaking from the body, not a fitting
  • Unit is 10+ years old
  • Repeated repairs on the same unit
  • Rusty or metallic-smelling hot water

A water leak that seems to be coming from the tank itself is sometimes actually a nearby supply line or fitting instead, which is worth ruling out with proper leak detection before assuming the whole unit needs replacing.
