Most water heaters in Union Township homes run 40 to 50 gallon tanks sized for a single-family household, and a typical unit lasts 8 to 12 years before efficiency drops and failure risk rises. When it's time to replace rather than repair, the main decision is usually tank versus tankless, and both have a real place depending on the home's hot water demand and available space.

We remove and haul away the old unit, install the new one to current New Jersey plumbing code, and pressure-test the connections before we consider the job finished.

Tank vs Tankless

Which Fits Your Home

Standard Tank

  • Lower upfront cost
  • Simpler installation, most homes qualify
  • Hot water limited by tank size
  • Typical 8-12 year lifespan

Tankless

  • Endless hot water on demand
  • Smaller footprint, wall-mounted
  • Higher upfront cost, often needs gas line upsizing
  • Typical 15-20 year lifespan

Older Union Township homes sometimes need a gas line upgrade to support a tankless unit's higher demand, which we assess before quoting so there are no surprises mid-installation.

Sizing It Right

Getting Capacity Right The First Time

An undersized tank means running out of hot water during normal household use; an oversized one wastes energy keeping water hot that isn't needed. We size based on household headcount, fixture count, and peak-use patterns rather than defaulting to whatever size was there before.

Installation Day

What To Expect

Most standard tank replacements complete in three to four hours, including old unit removal, new unit set, venting or gas line connection, and a full pressure test. Tankless installs, especially ones requiring gas line upsizing, can take longer.

Code Compliance

Why This Matters On Every Install

New Jersey's plumbing subcode is based on the National Standard Plumbing Code (NSPC), which sets specific requirements for water heater venting, temperature and pressure relief valve routing, seismic or tip-over strapping in some configurations, and expansion tank installation on closed water systems. An older water heater installed years ago, possibly before a given code update, doesn't automatically need to be brought up to current standard just for existing in place, but any new installation does need to meet the current NSPC-based code, and skipping that step is a common shortcut that creates problems at resale or during a future insurance claim.

We pull permits where required and schedule any needed inspection as part of the installation process rather than treating code compliance as an afterthought.

Extending The Life Of A New Unit

Simple Steps That Help

An annual flush to clear sediment buildup, checking the anode rod every few years on a tank unit, and keeping the area around the unit clear for proper airflow and combustion air on gas models all extend service life meaningfully. We can set up a simple annual maintenance visit at the time of installation if that's something you'd rather not have to remember to schedule yourself.
