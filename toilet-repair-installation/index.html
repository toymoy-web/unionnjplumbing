Toilet Repair & Installation in Union, NJ

A toilet that runs constantly, rocks at the base, or clogs repeatedly is usually a quick fix, but a cracked bowl or failing wax ring needs a real repair, not a temporary patch.

Toilet calls in Union Township split fairly evenly between running toilets wasting water, repeated clogs that point to a partial line blockage, and leaks at the base that usually mean a failed wax ring. Each has a different fix, and none of them require replacing the whole toilet unless the bowl itself is cracked or the model is old enough that parts are hard to source.

We carry common repair parts, fill valves, flappers, wax rings, and supply lines, on the truck, so most repair calls finish in a single visit. That applies equally to a call from Union Township or one from ZIP 07901 in Summit, where older toilet models sometimes need a less common part matched on the spot.

Common Problems

What Usually Brings People To This Page

Running Toilet

Almost always a worn flapper or an improperly adjusted fill valve, both quick, inexpensive fixes.

Rocking or Leaking At The Base

Points to a failed wax ring seal, which if left alone can let water damage the subfloor underneath.

Repeated Clogging

Can be a partial blockage in the trap or drain line, or in older homes a narrowing cast iron line better addressed with drain cleaning further down.

Cracked Tank or Bowl

Not repairable. Cracks in porcelain will spread, and replacement is the only real fix.

Repair or Replace

Making The Call

Most running-toilet and base-leak calls are straightforward repairs under two hundred dollars. Replacement makes more sense when the toilet is decades old, uses well above the 1.6 gallons per flush that modern fixtures use, or when the bowl itself is cracked. We will tell you honestly which situation you're in rather than upselling a repair-worthy toilet.

Installation

New Toilet Installation

Installation includes removing and hauling away the old unit, setting a new wax ring, and testing for a solid, leak-free seal at the base before we leave. We install standard two-piece toilets as well as comfort-height and one-piece models across all major brands sold in New Jersey.

Water Efficiency Considerations

Older Toilets vs Modern Fixtures

A toilet installed before the mid-1990s often uses 3.5 to 5 gallons per flush, compared to the 1.28 to 1.6 gallon standard on modern fixtures. For households in Union Township on municipal water and sewer billing, that difference adds up meaningfully over a year, and it's part of why replacement sometimes makes more financial sense than repeatedly repairing a much older unit even when the old toilet still technically functions.

Modern low-flow toilets have also improved significantly in flush performance since their early-2000s reputation for weak flushing, and most current models handle waste effectively on a single flush, which wasn't always true of first-generation low-flow designs.

A Note On Multiple Bathroom Homes

Staggering Replacements

For homes with several original toilets from the same construction era, we sometimes see all of them begin failing within a similar window, since they share the same age and wear pattern. Replacing them together in one visit is often more cost-effective than scheduling separate calls as each one fails individually, and we can quote multi-unit installation jobs accordingly.

If a bathroom refresh is already underway, it's worth checking the faucet and sink hardware at the same time, since matching fixture age often means matching wear.
