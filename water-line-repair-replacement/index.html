Water Line Repair & Replacement in Union, NJ

The main line running from the water meter to your home is a different job than interior plumbing, often involving excavation and coordination with the local utility.

The main water service line, the pipe running from the water meter or curb stop to the house, is a common source of leaks in older Union Township properties, particularly homes where this line is original galvanized or lead pipe from decades ago. A leak here shows up as a rising water bill, a wet spot in the yard along the line's path, or reduced pressure throughout the home even though interior plumbing is otherwise fine.

We diagnose whether the issue sits on the house side, which is the homeowner's responsibility, or between the meter and the street main, generally the utility's responsibility, before recommending a repair path.

Signs of a Service Line Problem

What to Watch For

Rising Water Bill

With no change in household usage, a common first sign of a service line leak.

Wet Spot in the Yard

Especially along the likely path between the meter and the house, even without rain.

Reduced Whole-House Pressure

A significant, sudden pressure drop across every fixture points toward the main line rather than a single fixture issue.

Discolored Water

Can indicate an aging or corroding service line, particularly in homes with original lead or galvanized service lines.

Repair Methods

Trenchless and Traditional Options

A localized leak found by pressure testing and acoustic detection can sometimes be repaired with a targeted excavation at just that point. For a line showing broader corrosion or repeated leaks, trenchless replacement pulls a new line through the old path with limited surface disruption, similar to the trenchless approach used for sewer lines.

Lead Service Lines

A Real Consideration in Older Homes

Some of the oldest housing stock in Union Township and neighboring older municipalities may still have lead water service lines, a material used before it was phased out for health reasons decades ago. If your home's service line has never been confirmed, replacement with copper or approved plastic piping is worth prioritizing over a repeated repair-only approach, given the health considerations involved.

Coordinating With the Water Utility

Meter and Curb Stop Access

Work on a service line often requires coordinating with the local water utility, particularly around the meter and curb stop, the utility-owned shutoff valve typically located near the property line. We handle this coordination, including any required utility notification or inspection, as part of the overall project rather than leaving it as a separate task for the homeowner to manage.

A Note on Pressure Problems

Not Every Pressure Issue Is the Service Line

Reduced water pressure has several possible causes beyond a failing service line, including a partially closed shutoff valve somewhere in the system, sediment buildup in older fixtures, or a pressure-reducing valve that needs adjustment or replacement. We check these simpler possibilities first before assuming a full service line issue, since a targeted fix elsewhere in the system sometimes resolves what initially looks like a service line problem.
