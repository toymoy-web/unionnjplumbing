Hydro Jetting in Union, NJ

If a line has been snaked before and clogs again within weeks, the problem is usually buildup coating the whole pipe wall, which jetting clears and snaking does not.

A cable snake punches a hole through a clog and gets water flowing again, but it does not remove the layer of grease, scale, or root fragments coating the rest of the pipe. That is why the same line often clogs again a few weeks later at roughly the same spot. Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure water stream to scour the entire interior wall of the pipe, which is the difference between a temporary fix and one that holds.

We use hydro jetting on both interior drain lines and main sewer laterals, adjusting pressure for the pipe material and condition so older cast iron common in Union Township homes is cleaned safely rather than damaged.

When Jetting Is The Right Call

Good Candidates For Hydro Jetting

Recurring Kitchen Line Clogs

Grease buildup that keeps narrowing the same section of pipe between cleanings.

Root Intrusion In Sewer Laterals

Jetting clears root mass from the pipe interior, often as a precursor to camera inspection and permanent repair.

Scale Buildup In Cast Iron

Interior corrosion scale that narrows the pipe diameter over decades.

Multi-Fixture Slow Drainage

When several drains run slow at once, the shared line likely has buildup along its full length, not a single localized clog.

What We Check First

Camera Inspection Before and After

For main line jetting especially, we recommend a camera pass first to confirm the pipe material and condition can handle jetting pressure safely, and a second pass afterward to confirm the line is actually clear rather than just louder-sounding.

Jetting vs Snaking

Not An Either-Or Every Time

Snaking is still the right tool for a single, obvious, localized blockage, and it costs less. Jetting earns its higher price on lines with a pattern of repeat clogging, since it addresses the buildup causing the pattern rather than just the immediate symptom.

Equipment We Use

Matching Pressure To The Job

Our jetting equipment runs at adjustable pressure specifically so we can calibrate for the pipe in front of us rather than running every line at the same setting. A newer PVC line can safely handle higher pressure aimed at heavy grease buildup, while an older cast iron line, especially one already showing some interior corrosion, needs a gentler approach that still clears the buildup without stressing a pipe wall that's thinner than it once was.

For commercial kitchens and larger multi-family buildings in our service area, jetting is often part of a preventive maintenance schedule rather than a one-time emergency fix, since grease-heavy drain lines in active food service settings build up again steadily over time regardless of how thorough a single cleaning was.
