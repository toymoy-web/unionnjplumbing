Faucet & Sink Repair in Union, NJ

A dripping faucet, a leaking shutoff valve under the sink, or a fixture that's simply worn out are all straightforward calls, usually resolved in a single visit.

Faucet and sink calls in Union Township cover a fairly consistent set of issues: a dripping faucet from worn internal cartridge or washer components, a leaking supply or shutoff valve underneath the sink, or a fixture old enough that replacement parts are becoming hard to source. The shutoff valve specifically is worth mentioning, since a valve that won't fully close, or won't turn at all after years of not being used, is a real problem the first time you actually need to shut off water to that fixture in an emergency.

We diagnose the actual cause rather than defaulting to a full faucet replacement when a cartridge swap or valve repair will do the job for less.

Common Problems

What Usually Brings People To This Page

Dripping Faucet

Usually a worn cartridge, O-ring, or washer inside the faucet body, not a sign the whole fixture has failed.

Leaking Shutoff Valve

Valves that sit unused for years often develop mineral deposits or worn seals that cause leaking once turned.

Low Water Pressure at One Fixture

Often a clogged aerator or, less commonly, a partially closed shutoff valve rather than a whole-house pressure issue.

Loose or Wobbly Faucet

Usually a loosened mounting nut underneath the sink, a quick fix rather than a sign of fixture failure.

Why Shutoff Valves Matter

A Small Part With Real Consequences

Every sink, toilet, and appliance connection has its own shutoff valve, and in older Union Township homes these valves sometimes haven't been turned in a decade or more. A valve that's seized or leaking is easy to overlook until the exact moment you need it, typically during an active leak elsewhere in the fixture. We test and, where needed, replace these valves as part of routine faucet and sink service, not just when they're already actively leaking.

Repair or Replace

Making The Call On An Older Faucet

Most drips and minor leaks are repairable with common cartridge and washer parts we carry on the truck. Replacement makes more sense for a faucet old enough that parts are discontinued, or one that's failed at multiple points over time, since a bundle of small repairs eventually costs more than a single new fixture.

Sink and Countertop Considerations

What Comes Up During Installation

Replacing a faucet sometimes reveals that the sink itself, or the countertop cutout it sits in, doesn't match a new fixture's mounting configuration without an adapter plate or minor modification. This is more common in older Union Township kitchens and bathrooms where the original fixture predates today's standard mounting hole spacing. We check this during the estimate rather than discovering a mismatch mid-installation, and we carry common adapter plates to bridge older sink configurations with current faucet designs.

Preventing the Next Drip

What Actually Extends Fixture Life

Faucets in homes with harder water tend to develop internal mineral buildup that accelerates cartridge and seal wear, which is part of why the same fixture in a softened-water home often lasts noticeably longer. Periodically removing and cleaning the aerator, the small screen at the faucet's tip, also helps maintain water flow and reduces strain on internal components from mineral accumulation.
