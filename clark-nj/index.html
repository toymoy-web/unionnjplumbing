Local Watershed

Why Drainage Matters More In Clark

Clark was incorporated in 1864 and named for Abraham Clark, a New Jersey signer of the Declaration of Independence. Today it's a roughly 4.45 square mile Union County township built out mostly with post-war suburban single-family housing. Robinsons Branch, a real tributary of the Rahway River with headwaters near Fanwood and Plainfield, runs directly through Clark on its way toward the Robinson's Branch Reservoir, the largest body of water in Union County, before continuing through Scotch Plains, Westfield, and Rahway itself.

That watershed geography means basements in parts of Clark, particularly homes closer to the Robinsons Branch corridor, see a real risk of groundwater intrusion and sump pump strain during heavy Mid-Atlantic rain events. It's a genuinely different local risk profile than the older, denser housing stock in East Orange or Orange, where drainage concerns are more about aging municipal stormwater systems than a specific waterway running through town.

Oak Ridge Park, a 90-acre county park on Clark's border with Edison, sits along part of this same watershed and is a useful local landmark for describing which part of town a service call is in.

A Smaller, Newer Township

How Clark's Housing Differs From Its Neighbors

At 4.45 square miles, Clark is one of the smaller municipalities in our service area, and its housing stock skews meaningfully newer than the pre-war construction common in Orange or East Orange. Most of Clark was built out during the post-war suburban expansion of the mid-20th century, which generally means fewer of the original cast iron drain and galvanized supply line issues we see in older Essex County housing, and more straightforward PVC and copper plumbing systems.

That doesn't mean Clark is free of aging-infrastructure concerns entirely. Homes from the earliest phase of that post-war building boom are now themselves 60 to 70 years old. That's old enough that original water heaters, supply lines, and early sump pump installations are reaching or past the end of their expected service life across a real share of the township.

What We See Most Often

Common Clark Service Calls

Sump pump repair and installation is a genuinely higher share of our Clark service calls than in most of our other coverage towns, a direct reflection of the Robinsons Branch watershed running through town. Standard water heater replacement and drain cleaning make up most of the remaining call volume, consistent with a town of mostly single-family post-war housing.

We also field a steady share of calls tied to Clark's mix of older post-war construction reaching the 60 to 70 year mark, where original supply lines and early sump systems are simply due for replacement regardless of any specific incident prompting the call. A pre-storm-season inspection is one of the more common preventive requests we get from Clark homeowners specifically.

One ZIP, Full Coverage

07066

Clark runs on a single ZIP code, 07066, covering the entire township from the Robinsons Branch corridor near Oak Ridge Park to the residential blocks further from the watershed. Drain cleaning and water heater repair requests come from all over 07066 fairly evenly. Sump pump calls concentrate more heavily in the lower-lying sections near the corridor itself. We also cover the neighboring 07065 Rahway, 07016 Cranford, and 07076 Scotch Plains ZIPs for the same core services.
