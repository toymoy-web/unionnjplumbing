Local Context

Orange's Industrial Past and What It Means Today

Note for clarity: this page covers Orange, Essex County, New Jersey, separated from Newark in 1806 and once known as the hat-making capital of the United States, home to as many as 50 hat factories in the surrounding Oranges area during the industry's late-19th-century peak. It is unrelated to Orange, California.

Many of those old industrial buildings, including the former Berg Hat Factory, have since been converted into residential lofts, part of a broader pattern in Orange's Valley Arts District of adapting century-old commercial buildings into housing. That conversion history matters for plumbing: retrofitted industrial buildings often mix original cast iron and steel piping with newer sections installed during conversion, and the transition points between old and new plumbing are common failure spots.

Orange's broader housing stock, much of it multi-family construction from the late 1800s and early 1900s built to house the hat industry's workforce, shares the aging-drain and hard-water challenges common across Essex and Union County's older municipalities.

Ongoing Redevelopment

What's Changing In Orange Today

Orange has seen a real wave of redevelopment in recent years, with former industrial and vacant commercial properties converted into residential and mixed-use projects, much of it within walking distance of the city's four NJ Transit train stations. New businesses moving into these redeveloped corridors bring plumbing needs of their own, from commercial-grade fixtures to grease trap service for new food and beverage tenants, alongside the ongoing residential repair work in the surrounding older housing.

That mix of very old and freshly renovated buildings, sometimes in the same block, means a service call in Orange can mean anything from working around a century-old cast iron stack in a converted loft to installing modern fixtures in a recently gut-renovated unit. We approach each one on its own terms rather than assuming a one-size-fits-all plumbing profile for the city. New residents moving into these converted lofts are often encountering original 1900s-era infrastructure for the first time and appreciate a plain, direct explanation of what they're actually looking at behind the wall.

Common Local Calls

What Orange Residents Call About Most

Drain cleaning and sewer lateral repair top the list here, a direct result of how much of Orange's housing dates to the late 1800s and early 1900s construction boom. Water heater service is close behind, particularly in multi-family buildings where a single unit may serve several apartments and failure affects more than one household at once.

We also see a steady flow of calls tied to the Valley Arts District's ongoing loft conversions, where new residents in converted industrial spaces are encountering original plumbing infrastructure for the first time. Coordinating with a building's other unit owners or a homeowners association is sometimes part of scheduling a repair in these converted properties, and we're used to working within that structure. A brief walkthrough of what's original versus retrofitted is something we're happy to provide on the first visit, since most new owners in these buildings haven't had that explained to them before.

Postal Coverage

ZIP 07050

The city of Orange sits entirely within ZIP 07050, from the old Valley Arts District lofts to the residential blocks further out, and that's the single code our dispatch covers for drain cleaning, water heater repair, and everything else on this page. We also serve the neighboring 07052 West Orange, 07079 South Orange, and 07040 Maplewood ZIPs for the same core services.

Before We Arrive

Orange's mix of century-old multi-family buildings and converted lofts means a burst pipe can affect more than one household fast, so quick first steps matter.

1

Shut Off The Main Valve

Stops the flow of water so damage doesn't keep spreading while help is on the way.

2

Kill Power To Wet Areas

Cut power at the breaker to any area with standing water, never at the outlet itself.

3

Clear The Immediate Area

Move people, pets, and anything valuable away from the affected space.
