Tankless Water Heater Service in Union, NJ

Tankless units fail differently than tank water heaters. Most issues are electronic or scale-related, not a burned-out heating element.

Tankless water heaters solve the running-out-of-hot-water problem that tank units have, but they trade that for a different maintenance profile. Where a tank unit typically fails from a worn part or internal corrosion, a tankless unit's most common issues are mineral scale buildup on the heat exchanger and error codes tied to the flow sensor or ignition system. Union Township's municipal water has enough mineral content that unflushed tankless units here do show real scale buildup over a few years of normal use.

We service tankless units from the major residential brands sold in New Jersey, whether the issue is an error code on the display, inconsistent water temperature, or a unit that's simply overdue for its annual flush.

Common Tankless Issues

What Usually Brings People To This Page

Error Code on Display

Almost always electronic, tied to the flow sensor, ignition system, or a venting issue rather than a mechanical failure.

Inconsistent Temperature

Scale buildup on the heat exchanger reduces efficiency and can cause temperature to fluctuate under demand.

Reduced Flow Rate

A clogged inlet filter or scale-narrowed heat exchanger both reduce how much hot water the unit can deliver.

Unit Won't Ignite

Gas supply, venting obstruction, or an ignition component failure are the usual suspects.

Why Annual Descaling Matters

The Maintenance Tankless Units Actually Need

Manufacturers generally recommend an annual descaling flush, circulating a mild acidic solution through the unit to dissolve mineral buildup on the heat exchanger. Skipping this is the single most common reason a tankless unit's efficiency drops over the first few years, and in harder water areas it can eventually cause a full failure. Union Township's water hardness makes this a real maintenance item, not an optional upsell.

Diagnosing an Error Code

Reading The Unit Correctly

Every major tankless brand uses its own error code system, and we check the unit's specific manual rather than guessing from a generic troubleshooting list. Some codes point to something homeowner-fixable, like a blocked air intake, while others point to a component that needs professional replacement, and telling the two apart correctly avoids an unnecessary service call for a simple fix.

Gas Line Considerations

Making Sure Supply Matches Demand

Tankless units draw significantly more gas on demand than a standard tank water heater, and an undersized gas line is a real cause of ignition failures or reduced output, especially on higher-capacity units serving larger households. We check gas line sizing as part of any service call involving ignition or output complaints, not just at initial installation.
