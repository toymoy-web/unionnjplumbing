Gas Line Installation & Repair in Union, NJ

A suspected gas leak is a safety issue first and a plumbing issue second. New line installation and sizing work follows a slower, code-driven process.

Gas line work splits into two very different situations. A suspected leak, whether from smell, a hissing sound, or a gas company alert, is a safety-first emergency: leave the house, do not operate switches or open flames, and call from outside. New line installation or sizing work, like running a line for a new range, generator, or upgrading capacity for a tankless water heater, is a planned project done to New Jersey fuel gas code.

We handle both, and we're direct about the difference: an active leak call gets immediate priority, while an installation project gets the careful code-compliant planning it needs rather than being rushed. This is true whether the call comes from Union Township or from a home in Clark's 07066 ZIP.

If You Smell Gas

Immediate Steps

Leave The Area

Get everyone out of the house immediately, don't stop to investigate the source yourself.

Don't Use Switches Or Flames

Avoid light switches, phones inside the home, or any open flame, all potential ignition sources.

Call From Outside

Contact the gas utility's emergency line and us from a safe distance, not from inside the home.

Don't Re-enter

Wait for the gas utility or a professional to confirm it's safe before going back inside.

New Line Installation

Common Reasons For A New Gas Line

Adding a gas range where an electric one previously stood, installing a whole-house standby generator, or upgrading to a tankless water heater are the most common reasons for new gas line work in Union Township homes. Each requires correctly sizing the line for the appliance's BTU demand, factoring in what else is already running off the same gas meter and supply line.

Sizing and Capacity

Why This Isn't A Guess

Undersized gas lines cause real problems: appliances that won't ignite reliably, reduced output, or in worse cases, unsafe operating conditions. We calculate total BTU demand across every gas appliance on the line before recommending pipe size, rather than assuming the existing line has capacity to spare.

Licensing and Permits

What's Required

Gas line work in New Jersey requires a licensed plumber and, for most new line installations, a permit and inspection through the local building department. We pull the required permit and coordinate inspection as part of the project.

Detecting a Slow Leak

When It's Not Obvious

Not every gas leak announces itself with a strong smell. A slow leak at an aging fitting can produce a faint odor that's easy to dismiss, or in some cases no detectable smell at all if it's occurring outside near a meter. Signs worth taking seriously include a hissing sound near a gas appliance or line, dead vegetation in an otherwise healthy patch of yard near a buried line, or a gas bill that's risen without a corresponding increase in appliance use. Any of these, combined with even a faint gas odor, is worth a professional inspection rather than waiting for a stronger, more obvious signal.

Older Homes and Original Gas Piping

A Real Consideration in Union Township

Some of Union Township's older housing stock still runs at least partial original black iron gas piping from decades ago. While black iron itself is a durable, code-approved material still used today, connections and fittings installed decades ago haven't always aged as well. A home undergoing renovation or an appliance upgrade is a reasonable point to have the existing gas piping inspected for corrosion or fitting integrity rather than assuming it's fine simply because it hasn't failed yet.
