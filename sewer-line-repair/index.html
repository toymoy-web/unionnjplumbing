Sewer Line Repair in Union, NJ

A sewer line that backs up repeatedly, or a camera inspection that turned up a crack or root intrusion, needs a repair plan built around what's actually wrong, not a guess.

The most common sewer line call in Union Township starts with a backup that keeps coming back after snaking, which is usually a sign of a structural problem rather than a simple clog. Root intrusion at a pipe joint is the leading cause, since mature street trees common in this part of Union County send roots toward any moisture source, including a hairline crack in an aging clay or cast iron sewer lateral.

We run a camera inspection to see exactly what's wrong before recommending a fix. A localized crack with root intrusion is often a candidate for a spot repair rather than replacing the whole line, and knowing that up front changes both the cost and the timeline.

What Camera Inspection Finds

Diagnosing Before Digging

Root Intrusion

Roots enter at pipe joints and expand inside the line, catching debris and causing repeat backups.

Cracked or Offset Joints

Ground settling over decades can shift older clay or cast iron sewer lines out of alignment at the joints.

Bellied Pipe

A low spot in the line where waste and water collect instead of flowing, often the result of soil settling under the pipe.

Grease and Scale Buildup

Interior buildup that narrows the pipe over years, worse in cast iron than PVC.

Repair Options

Spot Repair, Lining, or Full Replacement

A single damaged section found by camera can often be fixed with a spot repair, excavating just that section rather than the whole line. Trenchless pipe lining is an option for a line with multiple minor defects but a still-sound overall path, installing a new interior liner without digging up the yard. Full replacement makes sense when the line has widespread damage, a severe belly, or is undersized for the home's fixture count.

A line clogged with grease or heavy scale rather than structural damage is often better served by hydro jetting first, since clearing the interior buildup sometimes resolves the backup without any excavation at all.

Local Factor

Why This Comes Up More In Union

Union Township's inner-ring suburb housing means many sewer laterals date back decades, often before trenchless repair methods existed as an alternative to full excavation. Homes near mature trees along streets bordering Hillside, Elizabeth, and the Springfield Township line see a real share of root-related sewer calls tied to that combination of old clay pipe and established tree roots.

Municipal vs Private Responsibility

Where The Line Between Yours And The Town's Sits

The sewer lateral running from a home to the municipal main is generally the homeowner's responsibility in Union Township, as in most New Jersey municipalities, while the main line itself under the street falls to the township or utility authority. A camera inspection helps establish exactly where a given defect sits, which matters if a backup is caused by a blockage in the municipal main rather than the private lateral, a distinction worth confirming before assuming the repair cost falls on the homeowner.

We document camera findings clearly enough to support that conversation with the township if it becomes relevant, rather than leaving a homeowner to guess where responsibility actually falls. A backup severe enough to have already reached the basement floor is a separate, more urgent issue than the line repair itself, and our water damage restoration line handles that side of it.
