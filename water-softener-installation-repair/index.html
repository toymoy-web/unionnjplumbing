Water Softener Installation & Repair in Union, NJ

Mineral scale on fixtures, dry skin after showers, and water heaters wearing out faster than expected are all classic signs of hard water worth addressing at the source.

Union County's municipal water supply carries enough mineral content that hard water symptoms are a real, recurring complaint across our service area: white scale buildup around faucets and showerheads, glassware that comes out of the dishwasher looking cloudy, and water heaters that need descaling or replacement sooner than their expected lifespan. A water softener addresses this at the source rather than treating each downstream symptom separately.

We install new softener systems sized to household water use and hardness level, and repair existing systems that have stopped regenerating properly or are cycling through salt faster than they should.

Signs You Have Hard Water

What To Look For

Scale Buildup on Fixtures

White or chalky mineral deposits around faucets, showerheads, and inside the water heater tank.

Reduced Soap Lather

Hard water minerals interfere with soap and detergent effectiveness, requiring more product for the same clean.

Premature Appliance Wear

Water heaters, dishwashers, and washing machines all wear out faster under consistent hard water exposure.

Dry Skin and Hair

A commonly reported symptom, though individual sensitivity varies.

Installation

Sizing A System Correctly

Softener sizing depends on household size, actual water hardness level, and daily water use, not just square footage of the home. An undersized system regenerates too often and wears out faster; an oversized one wastes salt and water on unnecessarily frequent cycles. We test actual water hardness rather than assuming a generic county-wide average applies to every property.

Common Repair Issues

When An Existing System Needs Service

A softener that's stopped regenerating on schedule, is using excessive salt, or has stopped softening water effectively despite regular maintenance usually points to a failed control valve, a clogged resin bed, or a salt bridge preventing proper brine mixing. We diagnose which before recommending a repair or a full system replacement.

Salt-Based vs Salt-Free Systems

Understanding the Real Difference

Traditional salt-based softeners actually remove hardness minerals through an ion exchange process, the approach with the longest track record and the most consistent, measurable results. Salt-free systems condition minerals to reduce scale buildup without technically removing hardness from the water, an option some households prefer to avoid sodium addition or ongoing salt costs, though the results are generally less dramatic than true softening for households with genuinely hard water. We can walk through which approach fits your specific water hardness level and household preferences.

Bypass During Repair

Keeping Water Running

Most softener installations include a bypass valve, allowing water to flow around the system if it needs service or if a household temporarily wants unsoftened water for a specific reason, like watering plants sensitive to sodium content. We confirm this bypass is properly installed and functional as part of any service visit, since a bypass valve that doesn't work correctly can leave a household without water entirely during a softener repair rather than simply running on untreated water temporarily.
