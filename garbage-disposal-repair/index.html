Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation in Union, NJ

A disposal that hums but doesn't spin, leaks underneath the sink, or has stopped working entirely usually has one of a few common causes.

A garbage disposal that hums without spinning almost always means the flywheel is jammed, often by a piece of silverware, fruit pit, or fibrous food waste that got past the splash guard. It's usually fixable in minutes with the right tool, not a sign the whole unit has failed. A disposal that's silent and doesn't hum at all is a different problem, more often electrical or a tripped internal reset switch.

Leaks are the other common call, usually coming from a worn seal where the disposal connects to the sink flange or to the dishwasher drain line, rather than the disposal body itself.

Common Problems

Diagnosing By Symptom

Humming, Not Spinning

Jammed flywheel, usually cleared by manually rotating it with an Allen wrench through the bottom reset port.

Completely Silent

Check the reset button first; if that doesn't fix it, the issue is likely electrical, not mechanical.

Leaking Underneath

Usually a worn seal at the sink flange or dishwasher connection rather than a cracked disposal body.

Foul Odor

Often trapped food debris rather than a mechanical fault, though persistent odor after cleaning can indicate a partial drain clog downstream.

Repair or Replace

When A New Unit Makes More Sense

A jam or a worn seal is a straightforward repair. A disposal that's leaking from a cracked housing, has failed electrically more than once, or is old enough that replacement parts are hard to source is usually better replaced outright, since disposal units are not typically designed for extensive internal repair.

Installation

New Disposal Installation

We install standard and high-horsepower disposal units, matching the unit to household cooking habits since a household that processes a lot of fibrous scraps benefits from a higher horsepower motor than a light-use kitchen needs.

Compatibility With Older Kitchens

A Note For Union Township's Older Homes

Kitchens in Union Township's older housing stock sometimes have narrower under-sink cabinet space and drain configurations designed decades before disposals were standard equipment. Fitting a modern disposal unit into that space occasionally requires a plumbing adapter or a slight adjustment to the drain trap configuration, which we check during the installation estimate rather than discovering mid-job.

We also confirm the sink's electrical outlet, or install a new one where needed, since a disposal wired incorrectly or sharing a circuit with too many other kitchen appliances is a common source of nuisance tripping that gets mistaken for a disposal problem. A dedicated, properly grounded circuit resolves most of these nuisance-trip complaints permanently, and it's a fifteen-minute addition when done at the same time as the disposal install itself.
