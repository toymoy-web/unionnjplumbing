Sewer Line Replacement in Union, NJ

When a camera inspection shows widespread damage rather than an isolated defect, full replacement is often the more honest recommendation than repeated spot repairs.

Full sewer line replacement is a bigger decision than a repair, and we only recommend it when a camera inspection genuinely supports it, such as widespread root intrusion at multiple joints, a severely bellied section holding standing waste, or pipe material that's deteriorated broadly rather than at one isolated point. Union Township's older sewer laterals, particularly those still running original clay or cast iron pipe from before mid-century, are the ones most likely to reach this point after decades of service.

We walk through the camera findings with you directly, showing exactly what the footage found and why it points to replacement rather than another round of spot repairs, before any work begins.

Trenchless vs Traditional

Two Real Paths To Replacement

Traditional Open-Trench

  • Full excavation along the pipe path
  • Necessary when pipe path itself needs to change
  • More disruption to landscaping
  • Often lower cost for shorter runs

Trenchless (Pipe Bursting)

  • New pipe pulled through the old path
  • Minimal surface disruption
  • Works well when the existing path is sound
  • Often faster completion

Trenchless pipe bursting works by pulling a new pipe through the old one, fracturing the old pipe outward as it goes, which avoids digging up the full length of yard or driveway above the line. It isn't right for every situation, particularly when the pipe path itself needs to change or when nearby utilities are too close for safe bursting.

Permits and Inspection

What's Included

Sewer line replacement in Union Township requires a permit and inspection through the township, which we handle directly as part of the project rather than leaving it for the homeowner to coordinate separately. We also confirm connection point requirements with the municipal system before work begins.

What To Expect On Site

Timeline and Disruption

A traditional open-trench replacement typically takes one to three days depending on line length and depth, with landscaping restoration as a final step. Trenchless replacement often completes faster since it avoids full-length excavation, though access pits still need to be dug at each end of the run.

Coordinating With the Township

Connection Point and Right-of-Way

Where a sewer lateral crosses into the public right-of-way or connects to the municipal main under the street, work may require coordination with Union Township's public works department beyond a standard building permit, particularly if any portion of the excavation extends into the street or sidewalk area. We handle this coordination directly rather than leaving a homeowner to navigate two separate approval processes.

After Replacement

What Changes Going Forward

A newly replaced sewer line, whether PVC installed via trenchless method or traditional excavation, resets the clock on the age-related issues that likely prompted the replacement in the first place: root intrusion at aging joints, interior scale buildup, and offset sections from decades of ground settling. Modern PVC pipe doesn't eliminate the possibility of future root intrusion entirely, since roots can still find their way to a pipe's exterior joints over enough time, but it removes the compounding age and material factors that made the old line particularly vulnerable.
