Water damage in Union Township most often comes from one of three sources: a burst or leaking pipe inside the home, a basement flood from heavy Mid-Atlantic rainfall overwhelming the stormwater system, or backup from the Rahway River watershed that runs through and near the township. Union County has very little unincorporated land left and is one of the densest counties in the state, which means stormwater has fewer places to go during a heavy storm, adding real flood-adjacent risk to basements even away from the river itself.

Whatever the source, the response priority is the same: get standing water out fast, dry the affected area completely, and address the damage before mold has a chance to establish. This line is answered 24 hours a day for exactly that reason.

While You Wait For Us

What To Do Right Now

Shut Off Power To The Area If Safe

If standing water is anywhere near an outlet or panel, cut power to that area at the breaker rather than the outlet itself.

Don't Wade Into Standing Water

Especially if there's any chance it's in contact with an electrical source or the water may be contaminated from a sewage backup.

Move What You Can Safely Reach

Furniture and belongings clear of the affected area, without stepping into deep standing water to do it.

Photograph The Damage

Before any cleanup begins, for insurance documentation.

Why Speed Matters

The Mold Timeline

Mold growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours in a wet, unventilated space, which is why the gap between water intrusion and extraction matters more than almost any other factor in how much a job ultimately costs. A basement that sits with an inch of standing water over a weekend is a substantially bigger job than the same water removed within hours.

Union-Specific Flood Risk

Rahway River Watershed and Storm Drainage

Areas of Union Township near the Rahway River watershed see elevated basement flood risk during heavy Mid-Atlantic rain events, particularly in late summer and fall storm season. Dense, largely built-out neighborhoods with limited natural absorption add to the strain on local storm drainage during intense rainfall, which is exactly the risk a working sump pump is meant to head off before it becomes a restoration call.

What We Assess On Arrival

Categorizing The Water

Not all water damage is treated the same way. Clean water from a burst supply pipe is handled differently from gray water backing up from a washing machine drain, which in turn is handled differently from sewage backup, which requires additional sanitation steps before drying can safely begin. We categorize the water source on arrival since that categorization drives both the safety protocol and what materials can realistically be dried versus what needs to be removed and replaced.

We also check for water that has traveled beyond the visibly affected area, since water follows gravity and gaps in flooring or wall cavities in ways that aren't always obvious from the surface. A basement flood that looks contained to one corner has sometimes wicked moisture into adjacent framing or under flooring well outside the visibly wet footprint, and missing that during the initial assessment is a common cause of mold problems appearing weeks after a job seemed finished.
