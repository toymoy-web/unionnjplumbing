Slab Leak Detection & Repair in Union, NJ

A warm patch on the floor, water pooling near the foundation, or an unexplained rise in your water bill can all point to a leak under the slab.

A slab leak happens when a water line running underneath a concrete foundation develops a crack or pinhole, usually from age-related corrosion in copper pipe or from ground settling that stresses the line at a joint. In Union Township, this shows up most often in homes with slab-on-grade additions or garages, since the original portion of many older homes here was built with a basement or crawlspace rather than a full slab.

The classic sign is a warm spot on the flooring near where a hot water line runs, sometimes with a faint sound of running water when everything in the house is off. We locate the exact point of the leak before recommending a fix, since a targeted spot repair is often possible instead of breaking up the entire slab.

Detection Method

Finding The Exact Point

We use acoustic listening equipment tuned to the sound of pressurized water escaping through concrete, combined with thermal imaging where a hot water line is suspected. This narrows the leak to a specific location rather than an entire section of floor, which is the difference between a small access cut and tearing out an entire room.

Repair Options

Spot Repair vs Reroute

Spot Repair

Cutting a targeted section of slab at the confirmed leak point and repairing that section of pipe directly.

Reroute Above Slab

Running a new line through walls or ceiling instead of under the slab, avoiding demolition entirely when the layout allows it.

Which option makes sense depends on the pipe's age and condition elsewhere. A single isolated leak in an otherwise sound copper line usually favors a spot repair. A line already showing corrosion signs at multiple points often favors a reroute, since further leaks under the slab are likely.

Cost Factors

What Drives The Price

Slab leak repair cost depends heavily on how deep the pipe sits, how thick the slab is, and whether the leak is under a finished floor like tile that needs to be matched and replaced after the repair.

Why Slab Leaks Are Different From Other Leaks

The Structural Angle

Beyond the immediate water waste and utility cost, a slab leak left unaddressed can gradually erode the soil beneath the foundation, which over enough time is a real structural concern rather than just a plumbing inconvenience. This is part of why we treat a confirmed slab leak as worth scheduling promptly even when it isn't causing visible flooding, unlike a routine drain clog that can reasonably wait a few days for a convenient appointment.

Homes in Union Township with a slab addition, commonly a garage, sunroom, or later-added family room, are the most likely candidates for this specific type of leak, since the original portion of many older homes here was built with a basement or crawlspace rather than a full slab foundation. Standing water that's already reached the surface from a slab leak is a job for our water damage restoration line, run alongside the plumbing repair itself.
