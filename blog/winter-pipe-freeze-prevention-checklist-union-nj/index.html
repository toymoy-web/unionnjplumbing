A Winter Pipe Freeze Prevention Checklist for Union Township Homes

Published 2026-09-07 · Union Apex Plumbing

Union Township's humid continental climate means genuine winter freeze risk every year between roughly December and March, and the homes most likely to see a frozen or burst pipe tend to share a specific set of vulnerable points rather than freezing randomly throughout the structure. Walking through those specific points before the first hard freeze arrives is a more effective use of an afternoon than reacting after a pipe has already failed.

Crawlspaces

Unheated crawlspaces are one of the highest-risk locations in older Union Township homes, since pipe runs here are often directly exposed to outdoor temperature swings with minimal insulation. Check that crawlspace vents can be closed or sealed during the coldest months, and confirm any exposed supply lines have foam pipe insulation sleeves, an inexpensive fix that meaningfully reduces freeze risk.

Garages

An attached garage that isn't heated, but has a supply line running through it for an outdoor spigot or a laundry setup, is a recurring source of freeze calls each winter. If the garage door is left open for extended periods during a cold snap, that risk increases significantly. Insulating any exposed line and keeping the garage door closed as much as practical during freezing weather both help.

Exterior Walls

Pipes routed through exterior walls, sometimes the result of an addition or a bathroom placed on an outside wall during original construction, face more exposure than interior wall runs. If a specific faucet has frozen in past winters, that's a strong signal the pipe feeding it runs through an exterior wall with inadequate insulation, worth having assessed directly rather than hoping this winter is milder.

Outdoor Spigots

Exterior hose bibs that aren't disconnected and drained before the first freeze are a common, avoidable failure point. Disconnect garden hoses, drain the spigot, and if your home has an interior shutoff valve for exterior faucets, close it and drain the line before winter sets in. Newer construction sometimes includes frost-free spigots designed to avoid this issue, but older Union Township homes often don't have this feature.

During an Active Cold Snap

For the specific stretches of genuinely severe cold, typically single-digit or below overnight temperatures sustained for multiple days, a few temporary steps reduce risk further: letting a trickle of water run through the highest-risk faucet, since moving water is less likely to freeze than still water, and opening cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls to let household heat reach the pipes underneath.

If a Pipe Does Freeze

A fixture with no water flow during a cold snap, especially one on an exterior wall or unheated area, likely means a frozen line. Shut off the main water valve if you suspect the pipe may have already cracked, since a frozen pipe that's cracked internally will start leaking heavily the moment it thaws. Avoid an open flame for thawing given the fire risk near wood framing, and if you're not confident handling it safely yourself, that's exactly the situation worth calling a plumber for rather than attempting a DIY fix under time pressure.

The Cost of Prevention vs Reaction

Insulating a handful of identified vulnerable points ahead of winter costs a small fraction of what a single freeze-break emergency call runs, before factoring in any water damage from the burst itself. For Union Township homes with a documented history of freeze issues, having every exposed run assessed in a single visit is a more efficient approach than addressing each vulnerable point reactively across multiple winters.

Vacant or Lightly Used Properties

Homes that sit vacant for extended periods during winter, whether a vacation property, a home between tenants, or one undergoing renovation, carry elevated freeze risk since there's no regular water use or occupied-space heat helping keep pipes above freezing. If you own a property that will be unoccupied for any stretch of the coldest months, maintaining minimum heat, typically around 55 degrees, and having someone check on the property periodically are both worth arranging in advance rather than discovering a freeze break weeks after it happened.

A Final Word on Insurance

Keep in mind that a burst pipe from freezing is generally covered under standard New Jersey homeowners insurance, but insurers sometimes examine whether reasonable prevention steps, like maintaining adequate heat in the home, were followed. This is one more reason proactive prevention matters beyond simply avoiding the inconvenience of a burst pipe: it also supports a smoother claims process if a freeze event does occur despite reasonable precautions.

A Room-by-Room Recap Worth Printing Out

Before the first hard freeze of the season, a five-minute walkthrough covering the basement or crawlspace, the garage, any exterior-wall bathroom or kitchen, and every outdoor spigot is genuinely more effective time spent than most other single preventive tasks around an older Union Township home. Keeping this short list somewhere visible, a kitchen bulletin board or a phone note, makes it more likely the check actually happens each year rather than being remembered only after the first freeze has already caused a problem. A recurring calendar reminder set for early November is a simple way to make sure the walkthrough doesn't get missed before the season's first real cold snap arrives, and it's a habit worth passing along to anyone else in the household who might be the one home when a pipe eventually does freeze. Union Township's winters vary meaningfully from year to year, but the vulnerable points in a given home stay largely the same regardless of how severe any particular season turns out to be, which is exactly why a fixed annual routine works better than reacting to each winter's forecast individually, and it's a routine that pays for itself the very first time it prevents even one avoidable freeze break.
