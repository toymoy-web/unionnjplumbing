What to Do in a Plumbing Emergency: Shutoff Valve Guide

Published 2026-09-17 · Union Apex Plumbing

The single most useful piece of plumbing knowledge a homeowner can have isn't how to fix anything, it's knowing exactly where the relevant shutoff valve is before an emergency happens. Every minute spent searching for a valve during an active leak or burst pipe is a minute of continued water damage, and this is knowledge worth having ahead of time, not looking up for the first time during the emergency itself.

The Main Water Shutoff Valve

Every home has one main valve controlling all water entering the house, typically located where the main supply line enters the basement, often near the water heater or along an exterior-facing wall. In Union Township's older homes, this valve is sometimes an older gate valve that can be stiff or difficult to turn if it hasn't been operated in years, which is itself worth checking proactively rather than discovering during an emergency that it won't budge.

Shutting this valve off stops all water to the entire house, the right move for a major leak or burst pipe where you're not sure of the exact source, or when multiple fixtures are affected.

Individual Fixture Shutoff Valves

Most fixtures, toilets, sinks, and washing machines have their own dedicated shutoff valve, usually a small valve on the supply line directly beneath or behind the fixture. These allow you to isolate a single problem fixture without cutting off water to the entire house, which is preferable when you've confirmed the issue is contained to one specific location.

In older homes, these individual valves sometimes haven't been turned in years and can be seized or leak once operated for the first time in a long while. This is worth testing occasionally under normal conditions, not just discovering the valve doesn't work during an actual emergency.

The Water Heater Shutoff

Beyond the cold water supply valve at the top of the tank, gas water heaters also have a gas shutoff valve, and electric units should have a dedicated breaker. For a leaking or malfunctioning water heater, shutting off both the water supply and, for gas units, the gas supply, is the appropriate first step before calling for repair.

Outdoor and Irrigation Shutoffs

If your home has an irrigation system or exterior hose bibs fed by a dedicated line, there's often a separate shutoff for this system distinct from the main house shutoff, useful both for seasonal winterization and for isolating an outdoor leak without affecting indoor water.

A Simple Preparation Exercise

Walking through your home once and physically locating, and gently testing, the main shutoff and each fixture's individual valve takes perhaps twenty minutes and is genuinely one of the more valuable things a homeowner can do preventively. If a valve is difficult to turn or won't close fully, that's worth having replaced proactively rather than discovering the failure during an actual emergency when time matters.

What to Do After Shutting Off Water

Once water is off, the immediate danger of continued water damage stops, but the underlying problem still needs a real repair. Photograph any visible damage for insurance purposes, move valuables away from the affected area if safe to do so, and call for repair rather than attempting to reopen the valve to test whether the problem has somehow resolved itself.

Why This Matters More Than Any Specific Repair Knowledge

You don't need to know how to fix a burst pipe or a failing water heater to meaningfully limit the damage from either. Knowing where the relevant shutoff valve is, and confirming it actually works, is the piece of preparation that makes the biggest practical difference in how an emergency actually plays out.

Labeling Valves for Anyone Else in the Household

In an emergency, you may not be the one home to respond. A simple label or tag on the main shutoff and any less obvious individual valves means anyone else in the household, a partner, an older child, a house sitter, can respond effectively without needing to guess or search during a stressful moment. This takes only a few minutes with a label maker or even a marker and masking tape, and it's a small step that meaningfully improves your household's actual emergency readiness.

What to Keep Near the Main Shutoff

A small flashlight and a wrench sized for your specific valve, if it's not a simple hand-turn type, kept near the main shutoff location is worth the minor effort, particularly since plumbing emergencies don't schedule themselves for convenient daylight hours. Knowing the valve's exact location is only useful if you can actually reach and operate it quickly when needed, including during a power outage.

A Reasonable Annual Check

Building a quick valve check into an annual home maintenance routine, perhaps alongside checking smoke detector batteries or changing an HVAC filter, keeps this knowledge current rather than something learned once and forgotten. A valve that turned easily last year but has since seized from a small amount of mineral deposit is worth catching during a calm annual check rather than during the exact moment it needs to work. Pairing it with another task you already do regularly is the easiest way to make sure it actually happens every year.

Sharing This Knowledge Beyond Your Own Household

If you have elderly neighbors or relatives nearby, helping them locate and label their own shutoff valves is a genuinely useful favor that costs little time. It can meaningfully reduce the damage from a plumbing emergency they might otherwise be less equipped to respond to quickly on their own. This kind of practical, low-effort preparation tends to get overlooked precisely because it feels too simple to be worth doing deliberately.

In East Orange's multi-family buildings specifically, knowing which valve controls which unit is worth confirming with a property manager ahead of time, since shared systems make this less obvious than in a single-family home.
